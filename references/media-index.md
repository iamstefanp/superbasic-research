# Media Index — 98 Source Types

**Used at:** PLAN (Phase 3) — choose source classes deliberately across Paid, Owned and Earned.

## How to use this

Before you name a single source, name the source *classes* you intend to draw on, and make sure they span at least two of Paid, Owned and Earned. A pool built only from Owned sources is an organisation talking about itself — it will tell you what the subject wants said, never what is true. Earned sources carry the friction; Owned sources carry the claims; the contrast between them is where the finding usually lives.

The **Default Persona** column tells you what persona tag to expect when the source is read at INTEL. Use it as a prior, not a verdict — a "Varies" entry means the persona must be determined per item, and a mismatch between the expected persona and what you actually read is itself worth flagging.

## How to read a Paid source

A Paid source is not worthless and it is not a lie. It is **evidence of what someone was willing to pay to have said** — which is real information about position, priority and spend. It is simply not evidence that the claim inside it is true. Read it for the first thing; never bank it for the second.

Score Paid sources honestly rather than dismissing them. They are near-always low on Independence and low on Track Record — the buyer chose the words and, often, chose the outlet. But they can be high on Proximity (the buyer is closest to their own roadmap, pricing and headcount) and high on Specificity (ad copy and sponsored research name numbers that press coverage never gets). A Paid source scored 1/1/4/4 is a useful source with a known shape, not a discard.

A Paid source is a legitimate **Primary Artifact** when the research question is *about* the messaging — how a subject positions itself, what claims it is prepared to make in public, how it frames the market, where it places itself against named competitors. In that frame the paid placement *is* the evidence, and it is first-hand.

**Hard rule on independence.** A Paid source can never count as one of the independent sources supporting a CONFIRMED claim. A paid placement and the subject's own site share an origin — the buyer — so under the independence test they collapse to one source, however different the two mastheads look. The same applies to a sponsored analyst note and the vendor press release it repeats.

**Disclosure is the tell.** Clearly labelled paid placement is a source behaving correctly; note the mode and move on. Undisclosed paid placement — sponsored copy dressed as reporting, an unbriefed influencer post, a commissioned study published as neutral research — is a Track Record failure for the *outlet carrying it*, not only for the buyer. Flag it against both, and downgrade that outlet's Track Record for the rest of the run.

A Default Persona anywhere in this index is a starting guess, never the answer: tag what the source actually *is* in this instance.
