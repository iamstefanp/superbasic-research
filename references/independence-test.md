# The Independence Test

**Used at:** VERIFY (Phase 6) — before counting sources toward CONFIRMED.

This is the check most often skipped and the one whose absence most often
turns a confident report into a wrong one. Everything else in the method
can be done well and this one omission will still produce a fabrication
wearing a citation.

---

## What you are actually testing

You have five sources saying the same thing. **The question is not whether
they agree. It is whether they know it separately.**

Agreement is what triggers this test, not what passes it. Five sources
that agree because they are the same source are worth exactly one source,
and they are more dangerous than one source, because they feel like five.

Law 4: *Common origin is one source. Three articles from one press release
is one source wearing three hats.*

---

## The one-line test

For each source, ask:

> **If the original were wrong, would this source still be right?**

- **No** → dependent. It inherits the original's errors.
- **Yes** → independent. It found out some other way.

Everything below is how to answer that question honestly.

---

## The procedure

**1. Find each source's attribution.**
Read past the headline into the body. Look for: *"according to"*,
*"a spokesperson said"*, *"the report found"*, *"first reported by"*, a
wire credit (Reuters, AP, PA, dpa, AFP), a linked document. Most
dependence is declared in the text and simply not read.

**2. Name the origin.**
The origin is the earliest point the claim enters the record — the press
release, the filing, the interview, the dataset, the person who was
there. Not the article you read. Not the aggregator that surfaced it.

**3. Group by origin, not by outlet.**
Two sources with the same origin are one entry.

**4. Recount after collapsing.**
This is the step that gets skipped. You collapsed, so the number changed.
Compare the *new* count against the mode's floor — LIGHT needs 2
independent origins for CONFIRMED, HEAVY needs 3.

**5. If the count now fails, go back to INTEL.**
Not "note it as a limitation." Not "proceed with a caveat." Back to
Phase 4 (Law 10). A failing count is the gate doing its job.

**6. Record the collapse.**
The Independence Map in the VERIFY document shows which sources merged
and what the count was before and after. If you did the work and it
changed nothing, say that — a map with no collapses is a finding too.

## Five kinds of dependence

**Syndication.** The same content republished under different mastheads.
Wire copy is the common case. *Tell:* identical sentences, identical
numbers, a wire credit at the top or bottom.

**Common source.** Genuinely different articles, all worked up from the
same press release, report, filing or interview. *Tell:* the same
specific figure, the same quoted person saying the same words, a
publication cluster inside 48 hours.

**Citation chain.** A cites B, B cites C. Only C is a source; A and B are
transmission. *Tell:* the claim gets vaguer and more confident as you
move up the chain, and the original qualifier has been dropped.

**Institutional.** Same owner, same newsroom, same research group, same
consultancy. Shared editorial line, shared dataset, shared blind spots.
*Tell:* check who owns the outlet, or who the authors previously
co-published with.

**Circular.** A cites B; B cites A. Or a claim enters a reference work,
gets cited as established, and the reference work later cites the
citation. *Tell:* nobody in the chain has a primary attribution, and the
earliest instance you can find has none either.

---

## Fingerprints of a shared origin

Any one of these is a reason to look harder. Two together is near-proof.

- **An identical specific number.** Vague claims converge naturally;
  `£4.7m` does not. Precision is a fingerprint.
- **The same quote from the same person**, including the same portion of
  the same sentence.
- **A tight publication window.** Everything inside a day or two of one
  another, and nothing before.
- **Identical framing.** The same three points in the same order, or the
  same unusual adjective.
- **The same error.** Two sources repeating the same wrong date have
  the same parent. This is the single strongest signal there is.
- **No links.** A claim asserted with no attribution anywhere in the
  cluster usually means everyone took it from the same place and nobody
  wanted to say so.

---

## What genuine independence looks like

Independent sources differ in **how they came to know**, not just in who
published:

- One **witnessed** it; one obtained **documents**; one **interviewed**
  a participant; one **analysed data**.
- One has an **incentive to overstate** and one to understate, and they
  still agree on the fact.
- They **disagree on some details** while agreeing on the claim. Perfect
  agreement across sources is more often evidence of a shared origin
  than of a well-established fact.

---

## Hard cases

**Official statements.** An organisation's own claim about itself is one
source, however many outlets carry it. The outlets are not corroboration
— they are distribution.

**Data and analysis.** Five analyses of the same dataset are **one
source for the data** and five for the interpretation. Be explicit about
which you are counting.

**Academic literature.** Papers from the same lab or team are not
independent. Papers citing the same underlying study are not independent
on that study's findings. Follow the reference list, not the citation
count.

**Aggregators and reference works.** Never an origin. Use them to find
the origin, then cite the origin. A source you reached through an
aggregator but did not open is not retrieved (see the Reachability
check) and cannot support a claim.

**Republished under a byline.** A staff byline on wire copy does not make
it original reporting. Check for the wire credit in the footer.

---

## When you cannot determine the origin

Say so. An untraceable claim is not confirmed by sources you could not
follow back.

- Mark the claim **LIKELY** at best, never CONFIRMED.
- Record what you tried and where the trail ended.
- If it is a claim the whole report rests on, that is a gap, not a
  detail — it belongs in *Searched And Not Found*.

Failing to establish independence is a finding (Law 5). Assuming it is a
fabrication with extra steps.

---

## Rationalizations, pre-answered

*"Multiple sources confirm it."* — The exact phrase that launders a press
release into a fact. Confirm what, from where, first said by whom.

*"They're all reputable outlets."* — Reputation is not independence.
Five reputable outlets running the same wire story is one wire story.

*"Following it back takes too long."* — Then the claim is LIKELY, not
CONFIRMED. Mode is locked at the Brief (Law 7); the standard does not
fall because the work turned out to be harder than expected.

*"It's obviously true."* — Then the origin will be easy to find. Find it.

*"They can't all be wrong."* — They can, and if they share an origin,
they are wrong together. That is the whole reason this test exists.
