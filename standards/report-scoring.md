# Report Scoring: Evaluating Research Quality

## Introduction: From Source Quality to Report Quality

You've scored individual sources across the source-scoring dimensions
(see `source-scoring.md`) and recognized their personas (see
`source-personas.md`). Now comes the crucial final step: evaluating the
entire research report's quality based on its source foundation. A
report built on ten Gold-band sources might still be weak if all ten
sources are the same persona type saying the same things, while a report
mixing Silver and Bronze sources across diverse personas might be
stronger because it triangulates across different perspectives and
access points.

Report scoring isn't about counting points — it's about assessing
whether the source mix provides sufficient diversity, appropriate depth,
and adequate triangulation to support the claims being made and answer
the questions being asked.

## The Five Report Quality Dimensions

Every research report should be evaluated across five dimensions that
determine its robustness:

### 1. Source Diversity (Persona Mix)

**The Question: Does the report draw on multiple source personas, or
does it over-rely on a single type?**

Strong research triangulates across different source types because each
persona reveals different aspects of reality and has different blind
spots. A report based entirely on Primary Artifacts shows what the
protagonist wants known but not what's actually true; a report based
entirely on Independent Observers shows external perception but may miss
insider reality; a report based entirely on Anonymous Insiders reveals
unverified claims without authoritative grounding.

**Scoring:**

**5 = Excellent diversity** — uses 5+ different personas, each claim
supported by 2+ persona types, appropriate mix for the research
question. Example: business-field research combining Primary Artifacts
(operational signals), Independent Observers (market context),
Promotional Insiders (solutions landscape), Captured Experts
(benchmarks).

**4 = Good diversity** — uses 4 personas, most claims supported by
multiple types, minor gaps in coverage.

**3 = Adequate diversity** — uses 3 personas, key claims have some
triangulation, noticeable reliance on one type.

**2 = Limited diversity** — uses 2 personas predominantly, most claims
single-source type, significant perspective gaps.

**1 = No diversity** — dominated by a single persona, claims lack
triangulation, serious bias risk. Example: a report built almost
entirely on press releases and website content.

**Assessment method:** create a persona distribution table (count and %
of total sources per persona type, with the key claims each supports).
Example:

| Persona | Count | % of Total | Key Claims Supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Artifact | 12 | 40% | Operational priorities, stated strategy |
| Independent Observer | 8 | 27% | Market context, external perception |
| Captured Expert | 5 | 17% | Industry benchmarks |
| Promotional Insider | 3 | 10% | Solutions landscape |
| Anonymous Insider | 2 | 7% | Culture signals |
| **Total** | **30** | **100%** | |

---

### 2. Source Quality (Band Distribution)

**The Question: Are the sources individually strong, or does the report
rely on weak sources?**

A report with excellent diversity across personas can still be weak if
those sources are all Bronze or Questionable band. You need not just
different perspectives, but high-quality examples of each perspective.

**Scoring:**

**5 = Excellent quality** — 70%+ Gold/Silver sources, Gold sources
support all key claims, Bronze used only for background/context, no
Questionable sources cited.

**4 = Good quality** — 60–69% Gold/Silver, most key claims supported by
Gold/Silver, Bronze limited and clearly flagged.

**3 = Adequate quality** — 50–59% Gold/Silver, key claims have
Gold/Silver support, Bronze used more extensively, Questionable present
but limited.

**2 = Limited quality** — 40–49% Gold/Silver, some key claims rely on
Bronze, Questionable more prominent.

**1 = Poor quality** — <40% Gold/Silver, key claims rely on
Bronze/Questionable, quality concerns throughout.

**Assessment method:** calculate band distribution. Example:

| Band | Count | % | Use Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold | 15 | 50% | Primary evidence, key claims |
| Silver | 10 | 33% | Corroboration, context |
| Bronze | 5 | 17% | Background, landscape |
| Questionable | 0 | 0% | — |

83% Gold/Silver provides high confidence. Bronze appropriately limited
to background. Score: 5/5.

---

### 3. Triangulation (Evidence Cross-Verification)

**The Question: Do multiple independent sources corroborate key claims,
or do claims rely on single sources?**

Even high-quality sources can be wrong, biased, or unrepresentative.
Strong research validates important claims through multiple independent
sources — ideally from different personas with different potential
biases. If all your sources agree but they're all the same type with the
same blind spots, you haven't actually verified anything.

**Scoring:**

**5 = Excellent triangulation** — every key claim supported by 3+
sources, sources come from different personas, sources have different
potential biases, contradictions are noted and investigated.

**4 = Good triangulation** — most key claims supported by 2–3 sources,
good persona diversity in corroboration, minor claims may be
single-sourced with appropriate flagging.

**3 = Adequate triangulation** — key claims have 2-source support, some
reliance on same-persona sources for corroboration, background claims
often single-sourced.

**2 = Limited triangulation** — key claims often single-sourced,
corroboration when present uses same persona type, significant
single-source dependency.

**1 = No triangulation** — most claims single-sourced, no attempt at
cross-verification, high risk of source-specific bias.

**Assessment method:** for each key claim, map supporting sources (see
`triangulation-mapping-guide.md`). Average the per-claim scores for an
overall triangulation score.

---

### 4. Recency (Timeliness of Source Base)

**The Question: Is the source foundation current enough for the
research question, or are you drawing conclusions from outdated
information?**

Timeliness requirements vary by subject matter (fast-moving vs.
slow-moving topics — see `decay-classes.md`), but every report needs
source freshness appropriate to its questions. A report about current
market dynamics built on years-old sources is weak regardless of source
quality, while a report about cultural evolution might appropriately use
sources spanning a decade or more.

**Scoring:**

**5 = Excellent recency** — source age matches subject decay class;
older sources explicitly flagged and explained.

**4 = Good recency** — most sources appropriately recent; occasional
reliance on older sources with justification.

**3 = Adequate recency** — some outdated sources without clear
justification; recency gaps in certain areas.

**2 = Limited recency** — significant reliance on dated sources; may be
drawing current conclusions from old data.

**1 = Poor recency** — extensively dated source base; serious risk of
outdated conclusions.

**Assessment method:** for the subject's decay class, calculate the %
of sources within the appropriate timeframe. Example, for a fast-moving
regulatory topic:

| Time Period | Source Count | % | Appropriateness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last 3 months | 12 | 40% | ✓ Excellent for current situation |
| 3–6 months | 8 | 27% | ✓ Good for recent developments |
| 6–12 months | 5 | 17% | ⚠ Context only, not current state |
| 1–2 years | 3 | 10% | ⚠ Historical context only |
| 2+ years | 2 | 7% | ⚠ Background only |

67% <6mo is good but not excellent for a fast decay-class subject. The
older sources should be explicitly flagged as context rather than
current state. Score: 4/5.

---

### 5. Depth (Comprehensiveness of Coverage)

**The Question: Does the report have sufficient source depth to support
its scope, or is it making broad claims from thin evidence?**

Depth relates to whether you've investigated thoroughly enough given the
questions you're answering and the claims you're making. A report making
narrow claims about a specific subject might need only 15–20 sources if
they're well-chosen and thoroughly analyzed, while a report making broad
claims about industry trends might need 50+ sources to support that
scope.

**Scoring:**

**5 = Excellent depth** — source count matches claim scope, multiple
sources per key topic area, sufficient evidence for breadth of
conclusions, has sought contrary evidence.

**4 = Good depth** — generally sufficient sources, most topics
well-covered, minor gaps in specific areas.

**3 = Adequate depth** — minimum sufficient coverage, some topics
thinly sourced, claims sometimes outrun evidence.

**2 = Limited depth** — insufficient sources for scope, many topics
single-sourced, broad claims from narrow evidence.

**1 = Shallow** — clearly insufficient sources, claims far exceed
evidence, superficial treatment.

**Assessment method:** calculate the source-per-claim ratio (total
sources ÷ key claims), then assess coverage distribution by research
area:

| Research Area | Key Claims | Sources Used | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business field | 5 | 15 | 3:1 ✓ |
| Market field | 4 | 8 | 2:1 ✓ |
| Culture field | 3 | 5 | 1.7:1 ⚠ |
| Human field | 2 | 2 | 1:1 ⚠ |
| Brand field | 1 | 0 | 0:1 ✗ |

Good depth for Business and Market fields. Culture and Human fields
marginally sourced. Brand field claim is completely unsupported — either
add sources or remove the claim. Score: 3/5.

**Depth also means:** did you investigate counter-evidence, or just seek
confirming sources? Did you exhaust available source types, or stop
after convenient ones? Did you go deep enough into each topic, or skim
surfaces?

## Overall Report Score Calculation

**Method 1: Simple Average.** Add the five dimension scores and divide
by 5.

Example: Diversity 4, Quality 5, Triangulation 4, Recency 4, Depth 3 →
Average 4.0/5 = 80% = Strong Report.

**Method 2: Weighted Scoring** (if certain dimensions matter more for
your specific report type). Example weights for a regulatory compliance
report, where recency is critical:

- Diversity: 4 × 1.0 = 4.0
- Quality: 5 × 1.2 = 6.0
- Triangulation: 4 × 1.3 = 5.2
- Recency: 4 × 1.5 = 6.0 (weighted higher)
- Depth: 3 × 1.0 = 3.0
- **Total: 24.2 / 30 possible = 80.7% = Strong Report**

## Report Quality Tiers

**23–25 = GOLD TIER REPORT** ⭐⭐⭐ — excellent research with robust
source foundation, multiple perspectives, strong triangulation,
appropriate recency, and comprehensive depth. Conclusions are
well-supported and limitations are clearly acknowledged. Suitable for
high-stakes decisions.

**19–22 = SILVER TIER REPORT** ⭐⭐ — good research with solid source
foundation and adequate triangulation. Minor gaps or weaknesses don't
undermine core findings. Suitable for most decisions with awareness of
limitations.

**15–18 = BRONZE TIER REPORT** ⭐ — adequate research that answers key
questions but has notable limitations in source diversity, quality, or
depth. Suitable for preliminary analysis or directional guidance,
requires supplementation for important decisions.

**10–14 = QUESTIONABLE TIER REPORT** ⚠️ — weak research with significant
limitations. Insufficient source foundation to support conclusions
confidently. Should be treated as hypothesis-generating only, not as
basis for decisions.

**5–9 = REJECTED TIER REPORT** ❌ — seriously flawed research that
cannot be relied upon. Major gaps in source quality, diversity, or
depth. Conclusions are not supported by evidence. Requires complete
rework.

## Report Quality Examples

**Example 1: Strong Report (Gold Tier)**

**Topic:** Regional accounting firm e-invoice compliance readiness

**Source foundation:** 42 total sources. Diversity: 5 personas
represented in meaningful numbers = 5/5. Quality: 67% Gold/Silver = 4/5.
Triangulation: all 8 key claims supported by 3+ sources from different
personas = 5/5. Recency: 85% of sources from last 6 months for a
fast-moving regulatory topic = 5/5. Depth: 5:1 source-to-claim ratio,
comprehensive coverage across Business, Market, Human fields = 5/5.

**Overall Score: 24/25 (96%) = Gold Tier Report**

**Example 2: Adequate Report (Bronze Tier)**

**Topic:** Industry AI adoption trends

**Source foundation:** 18 total sources. Diversity: 3 personas, heavy on
generalists = 3/5. Quality: 56% Gold/Silver = 3/5. Triangulation: 3 key
claims have 2+ sources, 2 key claims single-sourced = 3/5. Recency: 67%
from last 6 months = 4/5. Depth: 3.6:1 ratio, but lacks expert voices =
3/5.

**Overall Score: 16/25 (64%) = Bronze Tier Report**

**Assessment:** Adequate for general landscape understanding,
insufficient for strategic recommendations. Lacks expert depth and
over-relies on accessible generalist sources.

## Appendix: Quick-Reference Scoring Grid

A condensed field cheat-sheet for the same five dimensions — use this
for a fast pass; use the full sections above when you need the worked
reasoning.

| Dimension | Diagnostic Question | Ideal Condition | Failure Mode |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Diversity** | Do we have multiple *perspectives*? | 4–5 personas | One-persona worldview |
| **Quality** | Are sources individually strong? | ≥70% Gold/Silver | Claims rest on Bronze/Questionable |
| **Triangulation** | Are claims independently confirmed? | 3+ sources from different personas | Repeated internal echo |
| **Recency** | Does time-horizon match decay rate? | Matched to subject tempo | Outdated inference |
| **Depth** | Is evidence proportional to scope? | ≥2 sources per key claim | Claims outrun evidence |

**Decay rate reference** (see `decay-classes.md` for the full
four-class version):
- **Fast** (regulatory, tech): 0–3 months ideal, 6 months acceptable
- **Medium** (market trends): 0–6 months ideal, 12 months acceptable
- **Slow** (culture, values): 0–2 years ideal, 5+ years acceptable

### Quick Diagnostic Checklist

Before finalizing any report, verify:

- [ ] **Diversity:** used 4+ personas?
- [ ] **Quality:** 70%+ Gold/Silver sources?
- [ ] **Triangulation:** key claims have 2+ independent sources?
- [ ] **Recency:** 70%+ sources within appropriate timeframe?
- [ ] **Depth:** 2:1 or better source-to-claim ratio?
- [ ] **Gaps:** coverage gaps acknowledged and flagged?
- [ ] **Contradictions:** conflicting sources investigated and
      explained?

**If you answer "no" to 3+ questions → Bronze Tier or below.**

**Field use:** score each dimension (1–5), sum for total (5–25), map to
tier. Document scoring rationale for the audit trail.
